I ran exactly the kind of seed-sensitivity check you described with LensKit’s `ImplicitMF` on `u.data`.

What the experiment did:
- Loaded MovieLens100K from `u.data`
- Converted it to implicit feedback by keeping ratings `>= 4`
- Used a fixed user-wise 80/20 train/test split with split seed `2024`
- Varied only the model random seed across 10 runs: `1, 7, 13, 21, 42, 87, 123, 256, 512, 1024`
- Trained the same `ImplicitMF` config each time:
  - `features=30`
  - `iterations=20`
  - `reg=0.01`
  - `weight=40`
- Evaluated top-10 recommendation quality with `NDCG@10` and `Recall@10`

Data actually evaluated:
- 43,929 train interactions
- 11,392 test interactions
- 942 evaluation users
- No test users were excluded after filtering

Results across seeds:
- `NDCG@10` ranged from `0.1633` to `0.1740`
- Mean `NDCG@10`: `0.1681`
- Std dev `NDCG@10`: `0.0034`
- Range `NDCG@10`: `0.0107`
- Coefficient of variation for `NDCG@10`: `0.0204`

- `Recall@10` ranged from `0.1859` to `0.1924`
- Mean `Recall@10`: `0.1889`
- Std dev `Recall@10`: `0.00185`
- Range `Recall@10`: `0.00655`
- Coefficient of variation for `Recall@10`: `0.00978`

Best and worst seeds in this run:
- Best `NDCG@10`: seed `7` with `0.1740`
- Worst `NDCG@10`: seed `1` with `0.1633`
- Best `Recall@10`: seed `7` with `0.1924`
- Worst `Recall@10`: seed `1` with `0.1859`

Interpretation:
- Yes, the random seed did affect results measurably in this setup.
- The effect was more noticeable for `NDCG@10` than for `Recall@10`.
- The `NDCG@10` spread of about `0.0107` is large enough that two runs with identical hyperparameters could look meaningfully different just because of initialization.
- `Recall@10` varied less, but still not zero.

Bottom line:
- In this experiment, reporting the seed is important.
- Even better would be reporting multi-seed averages (and ideally spread, like std dev), because a single run could overstate or understate performance depending on the seed.

The experiment’s own final interpretation was:
“Seed effects are noticeable enough that reporting random seeds or multi-seed averages is important.”

One limitation to keep in mind:
- This only isolates model seed effects for one fixed train/test split. It does not measure variation from changing the data split itself.
