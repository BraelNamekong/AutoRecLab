I ran exactly the kind of seed-sensitivity experiment you described on `u.data`:

- Algorithm: LensKit `ImplicitMF`
- Settings: `features=20`, `iterations=15`, `weight=40`, `method='cg'`
- Data split: fixed 80/20 train/test split with split seed `2025`
- Evaluation: top-20 recommendations on the same fixed test users for every run
- Metrics: `Recall@20` and `NDCG@20`
- Random seeds tested for training: `1, 7, 21, 42, 84, 123, 256, 512, 1024, 2024`

The split produced:

- 79,847 training ratings
- 20,118 test ratings
- 942 evaluable test users

What changed across runs was only the model seed.

- `Recall@20` ranged from `0.2905` to `0.2938`
- `NDCG@20` ranged from `0.2722` to `0.2837`

Average performance across the 10 seeds:

- Mean `NDCG@20` = `0.2763`

Variation across seeds:

- `Recall@20` std = `0.00118`
- `NDCG@20` std = `0.00345`

Relative spread:

- `Recall@20`: about `1.12%` of the mean
- `NDCG@20`: about `4.14%` of the mean

Best and worst seeds in this experiment:

- Best `NDCG@20`: seed `1` with `0.2837`
- Worst `NDCG@20`: seed `1024` with `0.2722`

- Seed effects were small for `Recall@20`
- Seed effects were more noticeable for `NDCG@20`, though still modest overall on this fixed split
- Reporting the seed is still important for reproducibility, because even with identical data and hyperparameters, the ranking quality changed by about `0.0114` absolute NDCG across runs
